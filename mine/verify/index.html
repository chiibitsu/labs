How the check works

For people, and for any agent

What a seal proves

A person was present at the device that holds this key, using Face ID, a fingerprint or the device PIN, and approved this exact list. And the list hasn't changed since.

What it doesn't prove

A legal identity. A seal says someone was there, not who they are. People shouldn't have to prove who they are. Only that they were there.

  1. Take every line that isn't blank, a # comment, or a Seal…: line. Trim each, join with a newline. That's the signed text.
  2. Hash it with SHA-256. Base64url of that hash must equal challenge in the seal's client data. If not, the list changed after sealing.
  3. Split Seal-proof on dots: authenticator data, client data JSON, signature (each base64url). Client data type must be webauthn.get.
  4. Verify the signature over authenticatorData ‖ SHA-256(clientDataJSON) with the public key in Seal-key (SPKI, ES256 or RS256). That's standard WebAuthn.
  5. Flag bits in byte 32 of the authenticator data: 0x01 means a person was present, 0x04 means they unlocked it with Face ID, a fingerprint or a PIN.

The code is about 150 lines, open, and has no dependencies: seal.js.
